GameStop makes $55.5B unsolicited offer to acquire eBay at $125 per share

Ryan Cohen proposes a 50/50 cash-and-stock deal to create what he calls "a legit competitor to Amazon." GameStop's market cap is $12B; it has lined up $20B in TD Bank financing to bridge the gap.

M&A E-COMMERCE MARKETS
TL;DR

GameStop offered $125 per share for eBay — a 20% premium to eBay's last close ($104.07) and 46% above where eBay traded in February when GameStop began accumulating its 5% stake. The deal is half cash, half GameStop stock, requiring $20B in bank debt plus GameStop's $9.4B cash reserve. eBay's market reacted with a muted 5% gain to ~$109 — far below the offer price — suggesting investors doubt the deal closes. Ryan Cohen plans to cut $2B in annual costs within 12 months.

¶01 WHAT HAPPENED

GameStop Corp. announced an unsolicited, nonbinding offer to acquire eBay at $125 per share on 4 May 2026 — a cash-and-stock deal structured as 50% cash and 50% GameStop common stock. The total implied value is approximately $55.5B.

The company has quietly accumulated roughly a 5% stake in eBay since February, primarily through derivatives. GameStop's own market cap is near $12B, making this a reverse-size acquisition: a buyer worth $12B attempting to purchase a target worth $46B. To bridge that gap, GameStop secured a $20B debt financing commitment from TD Bank and says it would tap its $9.4B cash reserve for additional funding.

Ryan Cohen — GME's chairman and the architect of the meme-stock era — framed the deal as a play to build "a legit competitor to Amazon," arguing eBay has a trusted seller ecosystem and marketplace network that GameStop's capital could activate. eBay responded that it is reviewing the proposal; its board has not endorsed it.

¶02 WHY IT MATTERS

The market's muted response ($109 vs. $125 offer) is the clearest signal that institutional investors view this as unlikely to close. The financing gap is real: the $20B TD Bank letter covers only part of the purchase; the rest depends on GameStop issuing new shares at current prices, which would dilute existing holders significantly.

What this story reveals regardless of outcome: GameStop has transformed into a vehicle for aggressive capital deployment, and Ryan Cohen is willing to attempt transactions at 4–5x his company's market cap. Whether that's vision or hubris will be answered by whether he can convince eBay's board to engage — or launch a hostile tender offer to shareholders directly.

¶03 WHAT TO WATCH
  • Watch eBay's board response deadline — GameStop indicated it would consider a hostile tender offer directly to shareholders if the board declines to engage. A hostile bid would require eBay's shareholders to decide directly, which is a much messier and more public process.
  • Track eBay's stock price relative to the $125 offer: if it closes above $115, arbitrageurs believe the deal has a reasonable probability of closing. The current $109 implies roughly 20–25% skepticism-adjusted probability.
  • Ask whether eBay's $2.4B marketing budget and flat buyer growth make it structurally undervalued regardless of this deal — Cohen's core thesis may be right even if the acquisition fails.
