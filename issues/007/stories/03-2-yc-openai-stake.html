YC's 0.6% stake in OpenAI is worth over $5B — a financial conflict that wasn't disclosed when Paul Graham defended Sam Altman

AI INDUSTRY OPENAI CONFLICT OF INTEREST
THE BRIEF

Y Combinator owns approximately 0.6% of OpenAI — seeded through YC Research in 2016 when Sam Altman was running YC before becoming OpenAI's CEO. At OpenAI's $852B valuation, that stake is worth over $5B. John Gruber's analysis argues this financial interest was not disclosed in the New Yorker piece that quoted Paul Graham defending Altman's trustworthiness — making Graham's defense structurally less credible than it appeared.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Gruber's point is not that Graham is lying — it's that a multi-billion-dollar financial stake is material information when evaluating a public character endorsement. Readers weighing Graham's views on Altman's trustworthiness deserved to know that Graham and his wife have billions at stake in OpenAI's success.

The New Yorker piece did not mention the YC-OpenAI financial relationship. Graham's published response focused on procedural claims ("we didn't remove Sam") rather than directly addressing the trustworthiness question — a distinction Gruber flags as telling.

WHAT TO WATCH

  • Ask, when reading any commentary on OpenAI, whether the author has disclosed financial relationships with the company — this applies to media, investors, and former advisors equally.
  • Watch whether the New Yorker adds an editor's note disclosing the YC financial relationship retroactively — that would be the standard journalistic correction here.
