SECURITY THREAT INTEL

Microsoft disrupts Fox Tempest — the malware-signing-as-a-service ring behind 16,000 signed samples

Microsoft’s Digital Crimes Unit seized infrastructure used by Fox Tempest, a threat actor selling valid Windows Authenticode signatures for malware, evading Defender and SmartScreen at scale.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 2 MIN READ

★ WHAT HAPPENED

Microsoft’s Digital Crimes Unit obtained a court order on May 21 to seize 47 domains and 12 servers operated by the Fox Tempest threat group. Fox Tempest ran a subscription service that provided other threat actors with valid Windows Authenticode signatures, allowing malware to pass signature checks in Defender and SmartScreen. Researchers at Mandiant and Recorded Future attribute at least 16,000 signed malicious executables across 2024–2026 to the group’s infrastructure, with clients including ransomware affiliates and infostealer operators.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

Code signing as an abuse vector has been a known gap for years — Fox Tempest is the first publicly named group to industrialise it at this scale. The seizure removes current signing capacity but does not address the root issue: the Authenticode trust model has no mandatory revocation latency requirement, so signed malware can circulate for weeks before certificates are blacklisted. Security teams should treat valid signatures as a confidence signal, not a clearance — and review any detection rules that deprioritise signed binaries.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Microsoft will publish a list of certificate thumbprints associated with Fox Tempest signing operations — add these to your block lists before they appear in the next Defender update cycle. The disruption may temporarily reduce signed malware volume, but expect reconstitution within 30–60 days as clients migrate to alternative signing services. Mandiant’s report names two competing services still operational; watch for attribution on those to follow from CISA or law enforcement.
