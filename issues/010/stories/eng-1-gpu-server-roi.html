05 WHAT IT MEANS
3 TAKEAWAYS · LINKS
01

Utilisation is the only variable that matters

The capex vs. opex calculation only favours owned hardware above ~60% sustained utilisation. Solo developers and tightly scoped teams with predictable workloads are the right buyers. Orgs with variable compute demand should stay in the cloud or use spot instances to bridge.

02

The HBM shortage has changed the calculus

Cloud GPU spot pricing has risen 30–40% since late 2024 as AI memory demand crowds out LPDDR (covered in story 02.3). Hardware bought in the 2024 surplus window now looks underpriced. Buyers today face a 15–20% premium on the same hardware relative to 18 months ago.

03

Maintenance time is a real cost — model it

The author logged ~40 hours of unplanned maintenance over 18 months. At a $150/hr opportunity cost, that is $6K against the $31K savings — still positive, but the margin is narrower than the headline figure suggests. Budget 3–5 hours/month for any owned GPU server and run the maths before buying.

06 RUN YOUR OWN NUMBERS
BREAK-EVEN FORMULA

The break-even threshold depends on three inputs: your hardware cost (H), your monthly cloud equivalent (C), and your monthly operating cost (colocation + power + maintenance, O). The formula is simple:

BREAK-EVEN (MONTHS)

H ÷ (C − O)

WHERE H = HARDWARE COST · C = CLOUD MONTHLY · O = OPEX MONTHLY

If (C − O) is negative — if your operating costs exceed your cloud savings — you never break even. This happens when utilisation drops below ~55% or when colocation costs are unusually high.
