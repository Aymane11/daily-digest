Antigravity 2.0 launches at Google I/O with 1M-token context and full managed-agent API

Google’s agent-hosting platform doubles down on long-context tasks: 10 built-in integrations, free-tier expansion to 100 RPM, and a companion CLI ship alongside Gemini 3.5 Flash.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 2 MIN READ

★ WHAT HAPPENED

At Google I/O 2026, Google shipped Antigravity 2.0 — a major revision to its managed agent platform. The update raises the maximum context window to 1 million tokens (powered by Gemini 3.5 Flash), adds 10 first-party integrations (Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Meet, Workspace, Maps, YouTube, Cloud Storage, BigQuery, and Vertex AI Pipelines), and opens a free tier at 100 requests-per-minute for developers building on the API. A companion CLI, the Antigravity CLI, was also released as an open-source tool for local testing and pipeline orchestration.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

Antigravity 2.0 converts Google’s long-context leadership from a benchmark claim into a shipped product: 1M tokens means an agent can hold an entire codebase, a year of emails, or a multi-document legal dossier in a single session. The 10 built-in integrations remove the most common reason teams build custom wrappers before they can test agent workflows. And raising the free tier to 100 RPM is a deliberate pressure on OpenAI’s Assistants API pricing — developers can prototype for longer before hitting a billing ceiling.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

The Antigravity CLI (filed separately as Tool 04.1) is the fastest path to test the 1M-token window locally before provisioning cloud resources. Watch for latency benchmarks on long-context tasks — Gemini 3.5 Flash’s 4× output speed gain means filling that window should no longer stall a user session. If you run Workspace integrations, audit which data Antigravity accesses before enabling the built-in Gmail or Drive connectors.
