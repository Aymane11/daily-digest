AI GOVERNANCE LABOR

600 Google workers sign an open letter urging Sundar Pichai to reject classified Pentagon AI work—the largest internal AI ethics protest since Project Maven in 2018

The letter demands Google refuse classified military AI contracts that lack guardrails against autonomous weapons and domestic surveillance. In 2018, a similar protest forced Google to exit the DoD drone imagery project.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 2 MIN

★ WHAT HAPPENED

On April 27, 600 Google employees published an open letter to CEO Sundar Pichai demanding the company refuse to enter classified Pentagon AI contracts without explicit guardrails against domestic mass surveillance and autonomous weapons without human control. The letter arrives as Google is actively negotiating a classified DoD contract that would deploy Gemini in classified settings. Google has proposed those same guardrail terms to the Pentagon—the identical conditions that led the DoD to drop Anthropic from its AI contracts in March 2026. The signatories explicitly invoke Project Maven, the 2018 DoD drone imagery contract from which Google withdrew after employee opposition.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

The Maven precedent is the relevant data point: employee protest worked in 2018. Whether it works again in 2026 will reveal how much leverage AI ethics internal advocates retain as competitive pressure from Microsoft and Amazon’s existing classified AI positions intensifies. The letter also signals that AI ethics concerns at large labs are moving from abstract policy debates to concrete contract-level decisions with measurable revenue stakes—a shift that affects how AI labs recruit, retain, and manage their workforces.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Ask your organization: does your AI usage policy include a position on military AI deployments by your model providers? If the DoD accepts Google’s guardrail language, your Gemini-based products operate under a provider that has committed to specific use restrictions—understand what those mean for your compliance posture. Watch whether the 600 signatories grow to a threshold that triggers management response, as it did in 2018.
