POLICY INFRASTRUCTURE

Rural America is cancelling AI data centers: 25 projects killed in 2025, Maine passes a statewide moratorium

67% of planned AI data centers target rural land. Residents near facilities face 267% electricity bill increases. Data center cancellations due to local opposition quadrupled year-over-year. A federal moratorium bill from Sanders and AOC is pending.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 4 MIN

★ WHAT HAPPENED

AI compute demand is routing hyperscale data centers into rural land where power is cheap and zoning is permissive—67% of planned facilities are in rural areas, versus 87% of existing ones in urban areas (Pew Research). Communities are now fighting back with regulatory tools. Maine became the first state to pass a moratorium on large data center construction until November 2027. At least 25 projects were cancelled in 2025 due to local opposition, a fourfold increase year-over-year. Studies show residents within the impact radius face electricity bills 267% higher than the national average, air pollution above national thresholds, and heat islands extending up to six miles. Sens. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez introduced a federal moratorium bill that would pause nationwide construction, adding regulatory risk across the entire asset class.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

AI capacity growth projections assume roughly 1,000 TWh of data center power by 2026. That trajectory now faces a structural constraint that balance sheets have not priced in: community veto power. Cancelled projects do not just delay AI training capacity—they raise the cost of the capacity that does get built (land, power purchase agreements, permitting) and introduce multi-year uncertainty. The electricity cost externality is politically actionable: a 267% rate increase is concrete, affects voters directly, and is easy for local legislators to act on. Infrastructure planners building AI capacity assumptions into product roadmaps need a material risk buffer for permitting delays and project cancellations.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Watch whether Maine’s governor signs the moratorium into law—if she does, expect five to ten other states to introduce similar bills within 90 days. Check whether the federal Sanders/AOC bill gains co-sponsors from red-state senators representing rural electricity consumers, which would signal bipartisan momentum. If you are evaluating AI infrastructure capacity contracts or data-center-adjacent real estate, apply a 20–30% project delay buffer for any rural US site not yet permitted.
