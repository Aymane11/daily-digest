POLICY AI GOVERNANCE

Google negotiates classified Pentagon AI deal as 600 employees sign letter urging CEO to refuse

Google proposes guardrails against domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons without human control—the same conditions that got Anthropic dropped from DoD contracts in March. Internal opposition echoes Project Maven in 2018.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 3 MIN

★ WHAT HAPPENED

Google is negotiating an agreement with the Department of Defense that would allow the Pentagon to deploy Gemini models in classified settings for “all lawful uses.” Google has proposed contract language barring its AI from use in domestic mass surveillance or autonomous weapons without appropriate human control. On April 27, 600 Google employees signed an open letter to CEO Sundar Pichai urging him to reject the classified contract. The backdrop: Anthropic was dropped from a DoD contract in March 2026 after requesting the same guardrails Google is now proposing. Google currently holds a non-classified DoD contract via its GenAI.mil platform and was the first provider to join the DoD’s new AI platform.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

The Anthropic precedent is the critical data point: the DoD rejected the same guardrail language that Google is now proposing. If the Pentagon accepts Google’s terms, it signals a shift in how the DoD interprets AI governance constraints. If it rejects them, Google faces the same outcome as Anthropic—exclusion from a classified contract that represents a significant revenue and strategic positioning opportunity. The 600-employee letter is the first major internal Google AI ethics protest since Project Maven in 2018, when employee opposition forced Google to withdraw from a DoD drone imagery contract. The question is whether that precedent holds or whether the competitive pressure from Microsoft and Amazon’s existing classified AI positions outweighs internal opposition.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Watch whether the DoD accepts or rejects Google’s proposed guardrail language—this will set the template for every subsequent AI-military contract negotiation. Ask your organization: if a model provider you rely on enters a classified government contract, does that change your risk calculus around model behavior or data handling? Evaluate whether your AI vendor agreements include transparency clauses that would notify you of material changes to how the provider deploys its models.
