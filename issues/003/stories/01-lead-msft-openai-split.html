Microsoft and OpenAI end their exclusive deal—OpenAI is now free to sell on AWS and Google Cloud

A $13B investment, 27% ownership, and six years of exclusivity just gave way to a multi-cloud future. OpenAI drops AGI trigger clause; Microsoft loses revenue share but keeps the IP license through 2032.

03 ¶01 · WHAT HAPPENED
TIMELINE
2019 First deal Microsoft invests $1B; exclusivity and revenue share begin
2023 $10B extension Azure exclusivity deepens; Microsoft resells GPT-4 to enterprise
FEB 2026 AWS conflict OpenAI signs $50B Amazon deal; Microsoft threatens legal action over Frontier exclusivity
APR 27, 2026 ← TODAY Non-exclusive license; AGI clause removed; OpenAI ships first on Azure but can sell anywhere

On April 27, Microsoft and OpenAI jointly announced a restructured partnership that dismantles the foundational exclusivity arrangement in place since 2019. The trigger was OpenAI’s February 2026 $50B Amazon deal, which granted AWS exclusive hosting rights for the Frontier agent-making tool—a direct conflict with Microsoft’s existing exclusivity terms. Rather than litigate, both companies negotiated a settlement. Microsoft gives up exclusive resale rights and stops paying revenue share to OpenAI. OpenAI gains freedom to serve customers on any cloud but continues paying Microsoft a capped revenue share through 2030. The AGI-trigger clause—which had given Microsoft the right to determine its response if OpenAI achieved artificial general intelligence—was also removed. Microsoft’s IP license, now non-exclusive, extends through 2032. Microsoft retains ~27% ownership of OpenAI’s for-profit entity regardless of cloud routing.

04 ¶02 · WHY IT MATTERS
3 PERSPECTIVES

This is the first major structural rupture in the AI infrastructure monopoly that has funneled enterprise AI spend almost entirely through Azure since 2023. Enterprises that built AI stacks locked to Azure’s OpenAI endpoints can now negotiate directly with OpenAI on any cloud. AWS and Google Cloud can now bid for OpenAI inference revenue. The removal of the AGI clause eliminates a governance risk that had given one corporation unilateral authority over the most consequential AI safety decision in history.

05 ¶03 · WHAT TO WATCH
3 TAKEAWAYS · LINKS
01

Check your Azure OpenAI contracts now

If your team has Azure OpenAI Service agreements, re-read the clauses around resale, exclusivity, and API routing. Multi-cloud OpenAI access is now permitted—negotiate at renewal. Ask your cloud account manager whether direct OpenAI API pricing is cheaper than Azure pass-through.

02

Watch for AWS and Google Cloud to launch OpenAI native integrations

Both clouds can now partner with OpenAI commercially without triggering exclusivity violations. Expect Bedrock and Vertex AI to announce OpenAI model access within 90 days. Evaluate whether multi-provider inference routing makes sense for your stack.

03

Monitor Microsoft’s own model investments for acceleration

With the revenue share cap and exclusivity gone, Microsoft has financial incentive to scale its own MAI model family (MAI-Transcribe-1, MAI-Voice-1, MAI-Image-2). Watch Microsoft Foundry announcements: expect aggressive pricing on proprietary models to defend Azure AI spend.
