PLATFORM MACOS

macOS 27 drops AFP file sharing and enforces TLS 1.2 minimum—dev beta June 8, GA mid-September

Apple finally removes the protocol it deprecated in Mavericks (2013). MDM, device enrolment, app distribution, and software update connections must meet ATS standards or break.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 2 MIN

★ WHAT HAPPENED

macOS 27 removes AFP (AppleTalk File Protocol) support entirely—a protocol Apple deprecated in 2013 when it moved to SMB3 as its primary file-sharing standard. Any Time Capsule or NAS device that does not support SMB3 will stop working for file sharing after the upgrade. Separately, Apple is enforcing stricter TLS requirements for server connections used by MDM, Automated Device Enrolment, app distribution, and Apple software updates: TLS 1.2 minimum, ATS-compliant ciphersuites, and valid ATS-standard certificates. TLS 1.3 is recommended. Content Caching servers are exempt. Developer beta ships June 8; public beta July 8; general availability mid-September 2026.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

Enterprise and education IT teams running on-premises Apple fleets have until mid-September to audit their MDM infrastructure, internal certificate authorities, and legacy NAS devices. A failed TLS audit means MDM enrolment stops working on day one of macOS 27 rollout—a hard blocker for device management at scale. The AFP removal primarily affects legacy hardware; any NAS purchased in the last five years almost certainly supports SMB3 already.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Run Apple’s network diagnostic logging profile against your MDM and enrolment endpoints before June 8. Check every internal certificate used for device management: confirm it meets ATS standards (valid chain, ATS-compliant cipher). Ask your NAS vendor if your model supports SMB3—if not, budget for replacement before September. Watch whether Apple publishes an official compliance checker tool at WWDC (June 8).
