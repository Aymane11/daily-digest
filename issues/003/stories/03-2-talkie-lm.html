RESEARCH MODELS

Talkie is a 13B language model trained exclusively on pre-1931 text—260B tokens, zero web data, built to study generalization without contamination

Books, newspapers, patents, and legal documents from before 1931. Anachronism classifiers filter out post-1930 leakage. The result: a contamination-free testbed for in-context learning, future anticipation, and out-of-distribution generalization research.

BY · EDITORIAL TEAM · 2 MIN

★ WHAT HAPPENED

Researchers released Talkie, a 13B-parameter model trained on 260B tokens of English text published before 1931—sourced from the Internet Archive’s books, newspapers, journals, patents, and legal documents. A custom OCR pipeline and anachronism classifiers ensure no post-1930 leakage. Post-training used historical texts with structured formats (etiquette manuals, cookbooks, dictionaries) and Claude-generated synthetic prompts with AI feedback to improve conversational ability. The model underperforms on standard benchmarks but matches modern models on fundamental language understanding and numeracy. Its primary use is as a contamination-free research testbed: testing whether a model with no knowledge of computers can learn Python from in-context examples, or how models anticipate future events from historical patterns alone.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

Most LLM evaluation is compromised by training data contamination—models may have seen benchmark answers during pretraining. Talkie offers a principled workaround: because its training corpus ends in 1930, any concept introduced after that date is genuinely novel to it. This makes it a clean instrument for studying in-context learning, out-of-distribution generalization, and the boundaries of what language models actually learn versus memorize. The approach also reveals how much of modern model capability is attributable to web-scale data specifically versus structured, high-quality historical text.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Try asking Talkie to reason about a concept it cannot have seen—provide a brief in-context definition and a task, then measure how well it generalizes. This is a direct test of in-context learning quality independent of memorization. Watch for follow-up research papers using Talkie as a benchmark instrument: if the methodology gains traction in evaluation circles, it could influence how future pretraining datasets are assembled and certified.
