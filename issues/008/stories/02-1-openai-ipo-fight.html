FILED · UNDER

OPENAI IPO FINANCE

OpenAI's internal debate about whether to list in 2026 or 2027 pits $852B ambition against $600B in spending commitments, missed user targets, and a CFO who says the company is not yet ready for the scrutiny of public markets.

¶ 01 WHAT · HAPPENED

CFO Sarah Friar has told colleagues she wants to delay OpenAI's IPO from Q4 2026 to 2027, creating the most consequential internal disagreement in the company's recent history. CEO Sam Altman, who has publicly positioned a 2026 listing as achievable, is pushing against that timeline. Friar's concerns are concrete: OpenAI missed its internal goal of one billion weekly active ChatGPT users, missed multiple monthly revenue targets, and has accumulated roughly $600 billion in future spending obligations for data centers and compute capacity. At the same time, competitor Anthropic reportedly reached $30 billion in annualized revenue — above OpenAI's $25 billion. Microsoft's exclusive licensing agreement with OpenAI has also ended, removing a structural revenue guarantee. Separately, Elon Musk's $150 billion lawsuit against the company continues.

$600B in future data center and compute spending commitments that must be serviced — regardless of when the IPO happens.
¶ 02 WHY · IT · MATTERS

An IPO at $852B valuation would be the largest tech listing in years and would set a benchmark for every AI company seeking public capital. If OpenAI delays to 2027, it signals that even the most valuable private AI company cannot yet demonstrate the revenue trajectory and operational discipline that public investors require. The $600B commitment figure is especially important: it represents future obligations tied to the Stargate infrastructure partnership with SoftBank, Microsoft, and Oracle, and means OpenAI must sustain rapid revenue growth not just to profit but to avoid an existential cash squeeze. The contrast with Anthropic — which reportedly has higher annualized revenue despite a lower profile — also frames a competitive dynamics question that public investors will examine closely.

"Going up a vertical wall of demand."

— SARAH FRIAR, CFO, PUBLICLY — WHILE PRIVATELY FLAGGING MISSED BENCHMARKS

¶ 03 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Watch for OpenAI's Q3 revenue figure, which will be the decisive data point in the 2026 vs 2027 debate. If annualized revenue climbs past $35B by August, Altman gains the argument. If it stalls below $28B, Friar's case becomes difficult to ignore. Also watch the Musk lawsuit: a settlement in either direction would remove one of the governance uncertainties that Friar has cited. Evaluate whether OpenAI files a Form S-1 draft with the SEC before November 2026 — that would confirm a 2026 timeline regardless of internal debate.
