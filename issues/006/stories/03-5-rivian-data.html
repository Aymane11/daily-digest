PRIVACY CONNECTED VEHICLES

Rivian's data collection cannot be fully disabled — eSIM removal requires a service appointment

Rivian confirms that core vehicle telemetry continues regardless of privacy settings, and that the embedded SIM — the hardware path for data transmission — can only be removed at an authorised service centre.

VIA · RIVIAN SUPPORT · 2 MIN READ

★ WHAT HAPPENED

A Rivian owner's investigation into the vehicle's data practices surfaced documentation confirming that Rivian collects vehicle location, speed, battery state, charging history, and diagnostic telemetry continuously and that the app's privacy toggles do not disable this collection — they only affect marketing preferences and certain feature-specific data shares. The embedded SIM (eSIM) provides the always-on cellular link for this telemetry. Rivian's support documentation states that eSIM removal or disabling requires visiting an authorised service centre, as it is embedded in the vehicle control architecture rather than user-accessible hardware.

¶ 01 WHY · IT · MATTERS

Connected vehicle data has become a significant privacy exposure vector — location history sufficient to reconstruct daily routines, workplace addresses, and social graphs is being collected and retained by manufacturers, and increasingly subpoenaed by law enforcement or purchased by data brokers. Rivian is not unusual here: Tesla, Ford, GM and others have similar architectures. What's notable is the clarity of the disclosure: the privacy toggles provide no meaningful control over the primary telemetry stream. For users with specific privacy threat models — journalists, activists, domestic abuse survivors — this is a material risk that the app UI obscures.

¶ 02 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Read Rivian's full data retention policy — specifically how long location and trip data are stored and under what legal standard they respond to government requests. If this is a concern, contact Rivian support and request a data export under CCPA or GDPR to understand exactly what has been collected. Watch for regulatory movement in the EU under the proposed Connected Vehicles Act, which would require explicit opt-in for continuous telemetry — the US currently has no equivalent requirement. Consider whether your threat model warrants raising the issue at purchase time, since eSIM removal post-purchase is a service-centre operation.
