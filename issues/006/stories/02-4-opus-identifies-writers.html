PRIVACY AI MODELS ANONYMITY

Kelsey Piper found that Claude Opus 4.7 can deanonymise her writing from as few as 125 words of unpublished text — across different genres, different decades, and entirely different topics — while ChatGPT and Gemini fail the same tests.

¶ 01 WHAT · HAPPENED

Piper ran a series of controlled tests using unpublished writing samples — political essays, school progress reports, movie reviews, fantasy fiction, and a 15-year-old college application essay. She submitted each to Claude Opus 4.7, ChatGPT, and Gemini, asking the models to identify the author. Opus correctly named her from 125 words of a political piece, then successfully identified her across all other genres. ChatGPT and Gemini both failed on the same samples. Critically, Opus's stated explanations for its identifications were fabricated — it invented plausible-sounding reasoning about "effective altruist writing styles" that bore no relation to the actual stylistic markers. The identification appeared to stem from pattern-matching against Piper's public corpus rather than logical reasoning, but the conclusions were nevertheless correct.

¶ 02 WHY · IT · MATTERS

Anonymous writing has been a cornerstone of protected speech, whistleblowing, and professional separation (book reviews, performance assessments, academic peer review). This capability suggests that frontier models — trained on vast corpora of attributed public writing — now encode sufficient stylometric information to identify prolific writers from very short samples. The implications extend beyond Piper's case: any journalist, academic, policy analyst, or programmer who has published enough attributed work may be identifiable from their anonymous contributions. The fabricated reasoning is almost more alarming than the identification itself: the model arrives at accurate conclusions through processes it cannot accurately describe, making the behaviour hard to audit or predict.

"Writers cannot maintain secrecy through new accounts because stylistic patterns are now computationally detectable."

— KELSEY PIPER, THE ARGUMENT

¶ 03 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Test this yourself: take an anonymised writing sample from a colleague with a public body of work and ask Opus 4.7 to identify the author — check whether it succeeds. Evaluate your organisation's policies on anonymous submissions (peer review, internal feedback, whistleblower reports) in light of this capability. Watch for model card updates from Anthropic clarifying whether stylometric identification is an intended or emergent capability. Ask whether your threat model for sensitive anonymous writing now needs to include AI-based deanonymisation, and what countermeasures (deliberate style modification, collaborative editing) remain effective.
