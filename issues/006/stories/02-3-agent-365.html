ENTERPRISE AI GOVERNANCE MICROSOFT

Microsoft launched Agent 365 as a generally available product on 1 May 2026, giving enterprise IT teams a single pane of glass to discover, govern, and audit every AI agent running across their Microsoft and third-party environments.

¶ 01 WHAT · HAPPENED

Microsoft's "Frontier Suite" launch on 1 May 2026 introduced two new products. M365 E7 — the first new enterprise licence tier since E5 in 2015 — bundles E5, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Entra Suite, and Agent 365 into a single $99/user/month SKU. Agent 365 is also available standalone at $15/user/month for organisations that already own E5. The product treats AI agents as "digital workers" with identities, policies, and guardrails managed through the same infrastructure used to manage employees: Entra for identity, Purview for compliance, and Defender for security. A central Agent Registry tracks every agent across Microsoft and third-party platforms. Audit trails, performance monitoring, and risk signal dashboards are included.

10M+ Agents already in the Agent 365 Registry after two months of preview access
¶ 02 WHY · IT · MATTERS

The 10M+ agents registered during preview illustrate the scale of AI agent sprawl already underway in enterprise environments. Without a control plane, organisations have no inventory of which agents are running, what data they access, or what actions they take. Agent 365 directly addresses the compliance gap that has slowed enterprise AI deployments — auditors cannot certify AI-generated work without audit trails. The E7 price point ($99/user/month) positions Microsoft to capture significantly more ARPU than E5 ($57/user/month) from organisations that are already paying for Copilot. The bundled structure also creates lock-in: once an organisation standardises on Agent 365 for governance, switching to a competitor's agent ecosystem becomes structurally harder.

"Agent 365 treats AI agents like digital workers, with identities, policies and guardrails."

¶ 03 WHAT · TO · WATCH

Evaluate whether Agent 365's registry covers your organisation's third-party agents (not just Microsoft's) before signing a new E7 deal — check the list of supported non-Microsoft agent platforms. Ask your Microsoft account team for a trial tenant to audit how many shadow agents your environment already has. Watch for Google Workspace and Salesforce responses — both need governance equivalents to stay competitive in enterprise AI contracts. Measure whether Agent 365's audit logs satisfy your compliance team's requirements for AI-generated actions before committing to full deployment.
